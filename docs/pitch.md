# Pitch talking points

Two to three minutes, honest framing.

## Opening (20 seconds)

Inquisitor checks whether keyboard and mouse timing on a paired PC still looks like the
person who enrolled. We only send numeric summaries, not what was typed.

## Problem (25 seconds)

Shared PCs and quick unlocks make it easy for someone else to keep working after you step
away. We want a continuous, consent-based signal—not a lock screen replacement.

## How it works (45 seconds)

You enroll through browser games or the tray agent. We train a personal model on your
timing patterns. During normal use, short windows are scored against that baseline. The
dashboard highlights sustained deviations.

## What we can show today (40 seconds)

- Pairing, enrollment, training, and live monitoring on a real Windows client
- Alerts with plain-language explanations on aggregate features
- Documented evaluation with separate sessions and reported sample sizes

## Limits we state plainly (30 seconds)

We do not read text. Controller input is out of scope. Our labeled pilot uses one genuine
user and consenting second-user sessions; it is not proof on arbitrary attackers. False
alarms can happen when you change tasks or keyboards.

## Close (20 seconds)

Inquisitor is a research prototype with visible pause controls and delete-my-data paths.
We are hardened enough to demo honestly and still learning where the model fails.
